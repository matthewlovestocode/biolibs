# Vegan cheese — where this got to

A working note, not documentation. Written 17 November 2026, corrected 19 November.

**The current record is [BENCH-PLAN-cheese.md](BENCH-PLAN-cheese.md).** This file is the snapshot of
where the biology stood before two days of corrections; read it for the standing facts and the
things known to be hard, and that one for what has happened since.

## The short version

The design work is done and shipped. Nine constructs for the four milk caseins, the enzyme that
curdles them, and the kinase that phosphorylates them — all computed from named public records and
read back from the finished molecule before being trusted. None of it has been made. The gap
between casein in a tube and curd in a pot is micelle assembly, which nothing here touches.

## Regenerated 18 November 2026

Every gene and construct in the lab was rewritten through the published
`@biolibs/bionet-tools@0.1.13`, using its `gc: "host"` option, replacing sequences that had been
made by a one-off script. The proteins are unchanged and the lengths are identical; the codon
choices differ. Checked afterwards: **20 of 20 molecules the library makes match what the lab holds,
by checksum.** The only checksums the proposals use that the library did not make are the three
published backbones, which are fetched rather than written.

The point was not better genes — the two sets scored the same on GC windows, sites, runs and
cryptic signals. The point is that the lab's contents can now be regenerated by anyone with that
package and the two host gene sets, instead of being taken on trust. Re-fetching the host genes
returned identical counts (327 E. coli, 211 Pichia), so that stage reproduces too.

**The PDF in `~/Downloads` is stale and was deliberately left so.** Its generator — the document
source, ten figure scripts, the captured screenshots — was lost when a session scratchpad was
cleared, and only the rendered file survives. What is actually wrong with it is narrow: every
length, name, form, figure and all the prose is still correct, and only the "first bases" column of
the two molecule tables (23 rows) and the sentence describing how the genes were written are out of
date. Rebuilding the generator to fix that is a few hours' work for a cosmetic column, so it waits
until the package is actually being sent. `method.json` beside it **is** current.

## The three results worth keeping

1. **FAM20C's motif reaches most of the phosphosites milk records across the four caseins, and CK2
   does not.** The rule was not fitted to the data — it is the published description of the enzyme.
   Re-derived from UniProt on 19 November in a script rather than counted by hand: **FAM20C 26 of
   31, CK2 12 of 31**, four matching neither. Those supersede the 27 and 14 first written here; the
   shape of the argument is unchanged and slightly stronger.
2. **Weighted by what actually binds calcium, FAM20C would deliver 97% of the phosphate a cow puts
   on; CK2 would deliver 54%.** Alpha-S1 alone carries 52% of the binding capacity and is the worst
   served by CK2 (5 of 9 sites). Kappa binds no calcium at all, so its phosphorylation can be
   ignored as a design concern.
3. **Yeast has no Fam20C.** The yeast constructs test an open question rather than implement a
   known answer. Supplying the kinase is its own project in the package.

## What is known to be hard

- **Proteolysis, and probably not only of kappa.** Published work on kappa-casein in Pichia found
  heavy degradation, periplasmic accumulation, extensive O-glycosylation and no detectable
  phosphorylation, attributing the degradation to kappa being intrinsically disordered. Caseins are
  the textbook disordered family, so expect the same for alpha-S1, alpha-S2 and beta. Protease
  knockouts are not an answer — yapsin 1 and yapsin 1/protease A both failed.
  **Measure intact protein, not total protein**, or a degraded prep reads as a failed experiment.
- **Micelle assembly.** Untouched, and the real endgame.
- **Kappa's sugars** will be yeast's, not a cow's, and those sugars are part of how it stabilises
  the micelle.

## What has happened since (19 November)

All of it is written up properly in [BENCH-PLAN-cheese.md](BENCH-PLAN-cheese.md); in one line each:

- **The casein in the co-expression construct had no tag**, so the one protein worth purifying could
  not be. Fixed, and the construct refiled as `pJTFlag-humancasein-CK2-His`.
- **The pPIC9K route was ambiguous.** XhoI cuts the vector twice, so the stated digest could close
  into two different plasmids of the right length and the check digest could not tell them apart.
  All five refiled on BamHI and AvrII: two fragments, directional, one product.
- **The products are circles now.** Every pPIC9K plan had recorded a line, which a ligation cannot
  make, and so had the empty vector.
- **The method was republished** and is coherent: 18 steps, nothing naming a molecule that nothing
  makes. The one published on the 18th is kept beside it as `method-2026-11-18.json`.
- **Nine annotated GenBank files** in `cheese-constructs/`, which is what a vendor or another lab
  wants to be handed.
- **Two checks shipped in the software** so the first two of those cannot happen quietly again:
  `orphaned` on a plan, and `checkRoute` in the tools.

## The next experiment, when someone has a bench

Two genes: one casein, one Fam20C. Same cell, selected on Zeocin against the casein's own marker.
Phos-tag gel, kinase-bearing strain against one without. Verify both markers are still present
before believing a negative — recombination between two cassettes can delete one silently, and that
failure looks exactly like the kinase not working.
